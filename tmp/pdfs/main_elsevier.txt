Abstract
Network-aware request scheduling for LLM inferencerouting each request
to the worker jointly best in compute backlog and link stateis widely re-
ported to improve tail latency, yet reported gains range from negligible to
over 70%, and one published system reports that its gains persist under
telemetry delayed by 60 s. We ask the prior question:          when does network
awareness pay, and when does trusting it hurt? Using a discrete-event
simulator with up to ten independent workload realizations per cell across
three sources (synthetic, the Azure LLM trace, and BurstGPT), plus a real
single-node k3s cluster, we report four ndings. (1) The benet is governed
by conguration, not link speed: it is −3.3% when the fastest-computing
worker also has the widest link and −68.2% when the two rankings disagree
                               −4
(Cli 's δ = 1.00, p = 1.8 × 10 ), persisting at 4, 8 and 16 workers. (2) The
two observed channels expire an order of magnitude apart: queue state loses
5685% of its value within 5 s, while link state retains 6589% at 60 s. This
explains the published  60 s does not matter observation, which swept only
the slow-expiring channel. (3) Per-channel discounting retains 9099% of
the benet under 60 s-old link state, where uniform discounting destroys all
of it; our own rst design is falsied by our own experiments. (4) On real
containers, a state read costs 56 ms to 876 ms against 12 µs for an in-cluster
round trip, grows with load, and staleness hurts in a threshold-like manner.
We also report what did        not work, including two refuted hypotheses. We
additionally anchor these simulator ndings with a real two-cloud LLM mea-
surement: bidirectional KV transfer completed successfully, but the observed
cross-cloud rst-token cost was substantially higher than the same-cloud split
baseline. This cloud result is descriptive and does not claim a universal scal-
ing law.

1. Introduction
     Large language model (LLM) serving has moved from single-GPU engines
to   disaggregated deployments in which prell and decode run on dierent
workers, KV cache travels between them, and each incoming request must
be routed to   some worker [1, 2, 3, 4]. Routing is no longer a detail: the worker
whose compute queue is emptiest is frequently      not the worker whose link can
deliver the request's context (or its KV cache) fastest, and the two criteria
can point in opposite directions. A growing line of work therefore makes the
router   network-aware : it scores candidates using both a compute-backlog
term and a link term [5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10].
     The problem is that this literature reports wildly dierent value for the
same idea. Reported gains range from negligible to more than 70%, and they
are usually quoted as a single number under a single conguration. Worse,
at least one recently published system reports a property that ought to be
alarming for anyone deploying this class of scheduler: its gains hold under
workload shifts and    telemetry delays up to 60 seconds  [9]. If stale network
telemetry does not matter, then a large fraction of the engineering eort
around network-aware routingand its sensitivity to monitoring latencyis
misdirected. Yet the same paper, and the closest concurrent work [8], sweep
only   one observable quantity.

Ten independent workload realizations, not one.. A single simulation of a
congested queueing system is not an experiment. In preliminary single-seed

paper aggregates ten seeds, uses per-request paired tests within a seed, and
quotes intervals over seeds. Comparisons that a single-seed screen would have

to be artifacts of one extreme seed (we give the concrete example in 7).

One mechanism variable, measured directly.. Instead of reporting that net-

whether the link term carries   any information beyond the compute term: the
rank correlation between per-worker compute capacity and per-worker band-

criteria select the same worker and the link term is redundant; when they
disagree, the link term is the only thing standing between the user and a 68%
worse tail. This distinctionwhich we call   alignment is, in our reading of
the literature, absent as an explanatory variable, and it accounts for a large
part of the variance in reported gains.

Staleness is not one thing but two, with dierent expiry times.. The sched-
uler observes two physically dierent quantities: the compute queue at each
worker (changes on sub-second to second timescales) and the state of each link
(bandwidth is a physical property, congestion changes on tens of seconds).
We sweep them jointly and nd their value expires at rates that dier by
roughly an order of magnitude. This single fact explains why the published
60 s does not matter claim is both true and misleading: it was measured on
the slow-expiring channel only. We further separate   latency (seeing an old
but true value) from   averaging (seeing a time-averaged value, where spikes
are smoothed away), and show the two distort decisions dierently.

2. Background and Related Work
2.1. Disaggregated LLM serving and KV movement
   Prell/decode     disaggregation   has   become   a   standard   abstraction:
compute-intensive prell and memory-bound decode are placed on separate
workers, and KV cache is moved between them over the cluster network
[1, 2, 3].   Production systems push this further with cache-centric global
schedulers [3], dynamic rescheduling and migration [4], phase-aware place-
ment [2], chunked prell to smooth the throughputlatency tradeo [11],
and locality-aware loading [12]. Because KV cache is large and its transfer
sits on the critical path, the network side of this architecture has become


                                       4
its own research area: KV compression and streaming [13], compression and
routing-collision avoidance, and network-aware placement of the two phases
[7, 14].   Recent position papers argue that the scarce resource has shifted
from raw FLOPs toward      managed state, placement, network movement, and
decision quality [15, 16]. This paper takes that shift as given and studies the
decision quality part: what the router must know, how fresh that knowledge
must be, and when acting on it is worse than ignoring it.


2.2. Network-aware scheduling
      Network-aware    scheduling   has   a   published   history,   mostly   outside
LLM serving.      CASSINI places training jobs using periodically refreshed
communication-pattern measurements [5]; VClos isolates tenants to avoid
ECMP hash collisions in multi-tenant GPU clusters [6];                TopoOpt co-
optimizes topology and parallelization [17].       For LLM serving specically,
Helix solves a max-ow placement problem over heterogeneous GPUs and
links [7], and GenSched jointly selects servers and allocates resources for
prell/decode stages in heterogeneous edge networks [10]. Concurrent work
closest to ours is NetKV, which selects decode instances using a network-cost
oracle over a modeled dynamic congestion eld [8], and Aegis, which places
work on an accelerator cluster using per-second switch queue and ECN
telemetry [9]. Two observations motivate our study. First, all of these works
evaluate a specic mechanism in a specic conguration; none characterizes
the   boundary at which the network term stops carrying information. Second,
none of them treats the correlation between the compute ranking and the
bandwidth ranking as an experimental variableHelix, for instance, solves
for a placement directly rather than studying how much the answer depends
on the two rankings agreeing.


2.3. Stale and delayed state information
      That stale state degrades load balancing is old knowledge: stale load data
leads to wrong server selection [18], delayed-information load balancing was
formally listed as an open problem [19], and stale link state causes routing
to oscillate [20].    ParSync turned admissible staleness into a design vari-
able by relaxing freshness to gain scalability [21].      The Age-of-Information
literature, however, warns against treating age itself as the objective:          no
universal staleness threshold exists, and the mapping from age to harm is
application-specic [22, 23]. Telemetry-side work optimizes freshness directly




                                          5
[24], and recent systems report that freshness-based guards can be far too
conservativeblocking 9295% of harmless cases in a dierent domain [25].
   Within LLM serving, NetKV derives a bound under which a stale es-
timate does not change the ranking, and reports a refresh-interval sweep
whose conclusion is that the benet is insensitive [8]; Aegis reports that
gains hold under telemetry delays up to 60 s [9]. Our results are consistent
with both, and explain them: both sweeps vary only the link-side observ-
able, which our measurements show is the slow-expiring channel (6).        We
additionally (i) vary the queue-side observable, which expires roughly an or-
der of magnitude faster, (ii) sweep the two jointly rather than one at a time,
and (iii) separate   latency from time-averaging, which are dierent distortions
with dierent consequences.      Finally, our own rst attempt at adapting to
stalenessdiscounting     within the link termwas falsied by our experiments
(7); we report it because the failure mode (muting the congestion penalty
makes fat link, slow compute workers look attractive) is easy to reproduce.

4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Simulator
   We use a discrete-event simulator built on SimPy. Each worker has si = 4
independent slots that advance at ri work units/s without contending with
each other (9), and one link pipe that serializes transfers.     A simulated
request goes through: (i) input transfer occupying the link pipe, (ii) compute
occupying a slot, (iii) output transfer occupying the link pipe. Transfer times
use the   true link state at the instant the transfer starts; routing decisions
use the   observed state. The simulator records, per request, the full time
decomposition (dispatch, transfer-in end, compute end, nish), which we use
for the white-box checks in 4.5 and for the real-cluster comparison in 8.
   Unless stated otherwise we use N = 3 workers with compute rates 16000,
8000, 4000 work units/s (four slots each; total capacity 112 000 work units/s)
and the link set {400 Gbps/1 µs, 100 Gbps/1.5 µs, 25 Gbps/2 µs}. The de-
ployed conguration attaches the slowest link to the fastest worker (ρ = −1,
anti), which is the interesting case; 5 varies the pairing deliberately. Two
network conditions are used throughout:       degraded (static, heterogeneous
links) and   unstable (bandwidth follows a mean-reverting process in log space,
θ = 0.5, σb = 0.8, σℓ = 0.4, sampled at 0.2 s). Static conditions are included
because they make link staleness exactly ineectivea degenerate cell that
we report rather than hide.

4.3. Load calibration
    Comparing policies across workload classes is only meaningful at the same
load intensity, since the classes dier by an order of magnitude in per-request
work. We therefore calibrate the oered rate per cell to a target intensity
ρ = 0.6 using the multi-resource feasible region : for each worker we take
the smaller of its compute-limited and bandwidth-limited capacity, sum over
workers, and set the arrival rate to ρ times that sum. Calibrating on aggre-
gate capacity instead is not safe: with heterogeneous workers the aggregate
gure can exceed the sustainable rate by more than 2×, producing a run
whose p95 only measures queue drainage.                Gate 1 (4.5) rejects any cell
whose realized load ratio exceeds 0.9; all cells in all three batches pass.

10. Conclusion
   Network-aware routing of LLM inference requests is usually reported as a
benet. We asked a narrower question  when does that benet exist, and
when does acting on network state make things worse?  and answered it
with a boundary rather than a speedup.


What we found..
    A conguration variable explains the magnitude.               When the
     fastest-computing worker also has the widest link, a network term has
     nothing to add (−3.3%); when the two rankings conict it is worth
     −68.2%. The eect separates completely across seeds and persists from
     3 to 16 workers. A network term's value comes from     disagreement, not
     from link speed.


    The two observable channels expire at rates that dier by
     roughly an order of magnitude. Link state retains 6589% of its
     value at a 60 s age; queue state loses 5685% within 5 s. This makes


                                     34
     the published observation that 60 s telemetry delay does not matter
     reproducible   and incomplete: it was measured on the slow-expiring
     channel.


    Averaging is not the same defect as lateness. Smoothing the
     reported state over a 30 s window inates absolute tail latency by 35
     52% on both policies, yet changes the relative advantage far less than
     the same amount of pure lag does. Degrading freshness and degrading
     resolution are dierent problems.


    Discounting must be applied at the level of the information
     source. Discounting within the link term (keeping how wide and
     muting how busy) is worse than not discounting at all: it re-creates
     the fat link, slow compute pile-up. Discounting per channel retains
     9099% of the benet under a 60 s-old link snapshot, where uniform
     and threshold discounting destroy all of it.


    The regime where discounting is necessary is one cell wide: a
     stale queue on a volatile network. Elsewhere full trust wins in 910 of
     10 seeds and discounting is a net loss. Our rule is therefore insurance,
     and we say so.


    Reading state is not free. On real containers, a state read costs
     56 ms to 876 ms against 12 µs for an in-cluster round trip, grows with
     load (an observation feedback loop absent from simulation), and polling
     imposes a oor of half a period to a full period on achievable freshness.
     The latency impact of staleness is threshold-like, with a knee between
     1 s and 5 s.
What did not work.. We report three failures of our own, because they are
the part of this study most likely to save someone else time. The rst dis-
counting design (term-level splitting) was falsied by our own experiments.
No discounting rule dominates the two extremes; the corrected rule is a net
loss in most conditions on one of the three sources. And two pre-registered
explanations for when does discounting pay  arrival burstiness and rate
persistence  were both refuted by the same data that motivated them,
so we leave the mechanism open rather than tting a story to it.      On the
real cluster, a promising observation (that stale state makes the network-
aware policy   more variable ) failed to replicate on an idle machine and was
withdrawn.


                                      35
What we would do next.. Four concrete experiments follow directly from the
gaps above. (i) Sweep the corrected rule along the load-intensity axis, which
is the largest hole in our coverage. (ii) Test the scale-matching hypothesis for
discounting value: does harm track the ratio between the staleness window
and the queue's own drain time?      The literature's report that a controller
whose timescale mismatches the machine's fails to generalize [46] makes this
concrete. (iii) Extend the testbed to multiple hosts with kernel-level shaping,
which is what would turn our direction-level cluster evidence into magnitudes.
(iv) Add an observation-error model  sampled, load-dependent age, with
timeouts and fallbacks  since the real cluster shows that both the variance
of age and the cost of measuring it are rst-order eects that a constant-lag
simulator cannot represent.


A closing observation.. The literature's disagreement about how much
network-aware scheduling is worth is not a contradiction to be resolved by
a better scheduler; it is a consequence of measuring the same idea under
dierent conguration alignments, dierent payload regimes, and dierent
observable channels.   Reporting a benet without stating which regime it
was measured in is precisely what makes those numbers incomparable 
and it is the omission that this paper tries to repair.


Real-cloud anchor.. The separate real-LLM measurements close the gap be-
tween the simulator and a deployed serving path without changing the pa-
per's main claim.   Same-cloud disaggregation increased median TTFT by
207.3% in serial execution and 97.1% at concurrency four, while total me-
dian latency increased by 16.0% and 9.8%. The two-cloud feasibility run
completed both directions with zero failed formal requests, but its median
TTFT was several times larger than the same-cloud split baseline. These are
descriptive anchors from four service rounds and two directions, not universal
statistical estimates; the dominant conclusion is that the KV path is feasible
but its network cost can overwhelm decode-stage gains.
