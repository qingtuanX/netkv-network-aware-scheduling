### 0.2 Abstract（draft）

Network-aware request scheduling for LLM inference — routing each request to the worker that
is jointly best in compute backlog and link state — is widely reported to improve tail latency,
yet reported gains range from negligible to over 70%, and a recently published system reports
that its gains *persist under telemetry delayed by up to 60 s*. We ask the prior question:
**when does network awareness actually pay, and when does trusting it hurt?** Using a
discrete-event simulator, ten independent workload realizations per cell, and three workload
sources (a synthetic generator, the Azure LLM inference trace, and BurstGPT), plus a real
single-node k3s cluster for direction validation and measurement, we report four findings.

**(1) The benefit is governed by a configuration variable, not by link speed.** When the
fastest-computing worker also has the widest link, network awareness yields −3.3% (essentially
nothing); when compute and bandwidth rankings *disagree*, it yields −68.2%. The two regimes
separate completely (Cliff's δ = 1.00, Mann–Whitney p = 1.8 × 10⁻⁴) and the gap holds at
4, 8 and 16 nodes. To our knowledge this variable — the rank correlation between compute and
bandwidth across workers — has not been used as an explanatory variable in prior scheduling work.

**(2) The two observed channels have order-of-magnitude different expiry times.** Queue state
(how busy each worker is) loses 56–85% of its value within 5 s, whereas link state (how wide and
how congested each link is) retains 65–89% of its value at 60 s. This asymmetry explains the
published "60 s does not matter" observation: that study swept only the slow-expiring channel.

**(3) A conditional, per-channel discounting rule dominates the two obvious extremes in the
regime where it matters.** Discounting both channels uniformly destroys the benefit
(−76.7% → +0.1% on the Azure trace at 60-s-old link state); discounting *within* the link term
— our own first design, which we falsify — destroys more than half of it (−76.7% → −40.2%).
Discounting *per channel* retains 90–99% of it. Meanwhile, on a volatile network with 5-s-old
queue state, full trust loses to the network-blind baseline in 4–6 of 10 seeds, and per-channel
discounting removes the harm (median +23.4% → −0.1%). A one-factor-at-a-time ablation across
all three workload sources shows the conclusion flips direction in at most 2 of 12 conditions.

**(4) On a real cluster we measure what the simulator omits.** Fetching scheduler state costs
56–876 ms, against 12 µs for an in-cluster network round trip — 50–900× — and it grows with
system load, forming an observation feedback loop. The latency impact of staleness is
threshold-like: negligible below ~0.75 s and 2–3.6× worse beyond ~3.6 s.

We also report what did **not** work: no discounting rule dominates at all staleness levels;
discounting is a net loss outside one regime; and two candidate mechanisms for "when discounting
pays" (arrival burstiness and rate persistence) are both refuted by our own data and left as
open questions. Over six repeat runs on the real cluster, only the fresh-state, moderate-load
cell has a stable direction, with repeat-to-repeat dispersion of 10–30 percentage points —
which bounds what a single-node cluster can claim.

### 0.3 Contributions（bulleted，正文 §1 末尾用）

1. **An explanatory variable for network-awareness benefit.** We introduce the rank correlation
   between per-worker compute and per-worker bandwidth as the variable that governs whether
   network information has anything to add; we validate it across 3 nodes × 10 seeds, across
   4/8/16 nodes, and against a direct probe of the alternative explanation.
2. **A two-channel model of state staleness with measured, order-of-magnitude-separated expiry
   times**, together with a 2-D (queue age × link age) sweep showing the two channels compose,
   and a separate characterization of *averaging windows* as a distinct distortion from latency.
3. **A conditional discounting rule** (`w = exp(−age_queue/4 s) · max(0.7, exp(−age_network/60 s))`)
   whose parameters are fitted from measurement rather than tuned, together with the
   falsification of the intuitive alternative and a three-source sensitivity ablation.
4. **A measurement of the observation feedback loop on real containers**, which the simulator
   abstracts away, including its threshold-like latency impact and the state-age floor implied
   by polling.
5. **A reproducibility protocol** (four gates, append-only artifacts, per-request paired tests,
   manifest per run) and an explicit list of the negative and zero results above.
